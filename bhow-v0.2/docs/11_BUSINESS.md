# 11 — Business thesis, distribution and responsible monetization

## Build a trusted utility before trying to monetize attention

**Customer hypothesis:** dog owners who already keep care notes in WhatsApp/phone gallery/paper cards, particularly first-time parents with recurring visits. **Economic buyer hypothesis:** first user may not pay, but a subgroup of multi-dog caregivers or participating clinics may pay for collaboration/verified handoff. The large total pet population does not equal our addressable paying customers.

## Positioning vs alternatives

| Alternative | Existing advantage | Why a user might switch to Bhow |
|---|---|---|
| Notes/WhatsApp/photos | Universal, zero friction | Timed actions, structured chronology, portable export, provenance |
| General reminders | Excellent push delivery | Context tied to one dog's health record |
| Breed identification tools | Fun discovery | One-time discovery leads into the dog's long-term needs |
| Existing pet care/health apps | Broad features, installed trust | Simpler, more localized UX and better handoff if validated |
| Clinic-specific apps | Clinic records with institutional trust | Care continuity across clinics and caretakers, opt-in |
| Community social groups | Strong local relationships | Accountable status changes and auditable welfare outcomes, later |

Do **not** claim we are the first or only platform; competitive research, paid interviews and positioning tests are required.

## Business-model ladder

**Stage 1 — Free care core (launch):** profiles, manually entered care records, basic reminders, essential food-hazard education, export, emergency help. Safety cannot be paywalled.

**Stage 2 — Optional consumer Plus (price experiment):** better family collaboration, more record history tools, advanced reminder coordination, document storage within sensible limits, visit preparation templates and personal yearbook. Candidate pricing to *test*, not validated: ₹79–₹199 per month or ₹699–₹1,499 per year. Do not charge based on emotional pressure or urgent risk.

**Stage 3 — Vet practice tools (pilot):** time-saving visit summary, patient-managed record requests, consented verification workflow, reminders with clinic-confirmed dates. Pricing requires a demonstrated saved-workflow benefit and at least one paid pilot. Never sell clinical influence to sponsors.

**Stage 4 — Welfare infrastructure:** sponsor-backed NGO case coordination, vaccination and sterilization programme tracking. Any public metrics must be traceable; NGOs do not automatically have a SaaS budget.

**Stage 5 — Transparent referral marketplace:** only after real demand, service verification and conflict-of-interest safeguards. Sponsored content must not influence safety advice or search ranking disguised as clinical recommendation.

## Unit economics model (assumptions, not forecast)

Monthly contribution ≈ paying subscribers × (ARPPU − payment fee − variable infrastructure − support) + verified partner revenue − fixed operating spend. Build a cohort spreadsheet only after first 30–50 real users. Primary unknowns: D30 retention, monthly care actions, willingness to pay, clinician uptake, photo inference costs, customer support hours, acquisition cost.

## Distribution plan — Hyderabad pilot first

1. Recruit 20–50 caregivers through trusted local dog communities and participating veterinarians; prioritize breadth (young, old, indie, pedigree, rescue context).
2. Offer a **free import-your-records session** at a small number of consenting clinics; show a tidy vet-ready summary.
3. Use breed visual resemblance as shareable top-of-funnel **only if accurate and licensed**, then invite a real care action.
4. Partner with one shelter/NGO later for a **closed** rescue case workflow, not an open location-sharing free-for-all.
5. Publish transparent impact notes: features that worked, incorrect predictions, user trust issues and the fixes.

## Testable differentiation

Hypothesis H1: “A beautiful shareable care passport” generates more repeat use than a breed-ID app. H2: “Upcoming appointment prep” has enough value that caregivers voluntarily enter records. H3: Partner vet confirmation builds trust enough to motivate clinic participation. H4: Ingredient guidance is used repeatedly, without making users overconfident. Define each hypothesis's success event, date, sample and failure threshold before promoting it.

## Trust-based moat (earned, not assumed)

High-quality permissioned longitudinal care histories and professional provenance create value only when sufficiently accurate, updated and portable. Never create lock-in by withholding export. Indian breed datasets must be licensed and scientifically labeled; superficial photo labels are not a moat. The defensible asset is the **trusted network and usable workflow**, not the raw personal data for resale.
